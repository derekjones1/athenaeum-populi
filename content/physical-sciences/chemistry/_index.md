Chemistry is the study of matter: what it is made of, how its structure
explains its properties, and how it changes. This book is the two-semester
general chemistry course for science, engineering, and health majors. It
starts with measurement, atoms, and the mole, builds the quantitative
toolkit of stoichiometry and thermochemistry, explains bonding and molecular
shape from the electronic structure of atoms, and then treats gases,
liquids, solids, and solutions. The second half covers how fast reactions
go and how far — kinetics, equilibrium, acids and bases, thermodynamics, and
electrochemistry — and closes with the chemistry of the elements, organic
chemistry, and nuclear chemistry.

## Chapters

- **Essential Ideas** — chemistry in context, the phases and classification
  of matter, physical and chemical properties, measurements, measurement
  uncertainty, and the mathematical treatment of measurement results.

## Appendices

The book's thirteen [appendices](/physical-sciences/chemistry/appendices/)
are complete: the periodic table, a review of the mathematics the book uses,
and eleven tables of units, constants, and data — among them the standard
thermodynamic properties, ionization constants, solubility products, and
standard electrode potentials that the chapters refer to by letter.

## Planned contents

The remaining chapters of Chemistry 2e will appear in this order:

- **Atoms, Molecules, and Ions** — atomic theory and its evolution, atomic
  structure and symbolism, chemical formulas, the periodic table, ionic and
  molecular compounds, and chemical nomenclature.
- **Composition of Substances and Solutions** — formula mass and the mole,
  empirical and molecular formulas, molarity, and other units for solution
  concentrations.
- **Stoichiometry of Chemical Reactions** — writing and balancing chemical
  equations, classifying reactions, reaction stoichiometry, reaction yields,
  and quantitative chemical analysis.
- **Thermochemistry** — energy basics, calorimetry, and enthalpy.
- **Electronic Structure and Periodic Properties of Elements** —
  electromagnetic energy, the Bohr model, the development of quantum theory,
  electron configurations, and periodic variations in element properties.
- **Chemical Bonding and Molecular Geometry** — ionic and covalent bonding,
  Lewis symbols and structures, formal charges and resonance, bond
  strengths, and molecular structure and polarity.
- **Advanced Theories of Covalent Bonding** — valence bond theory, hybrid
  atomic orbitals, multiple bonds, and molecular orbital theory.
- **Gases** — gas pressure, the ideal gas law, the stoichiometry of gaseous
  substances, mixtures, and reactions, effusion and diffusion, the
  kinetic-molecular theory, and non-ideal gas behavior.
- **Liquids and Solids** — intermolecular forces, properties of liquids,
  phase transitions, phase diagrams, the solid state of matter, and lattice
  structures in crystalline solids.
- **Solutions and Colloids** — the dissolution process, electrolytes,
  solubility, colligative properties, and colloids.
- **Kinetics** — chemical reaction rates and the factors that affect them,
  rate laws, integrated rate laws, collision theory, reaction mechanisms,
  and catalysis.
- **Fundamental Equilibrium Concepts** — chemical equilibria, equilibrium
  constants, Le Châtelier's principle, and equilibrium calculations.
- **Acid-Base Equilibria** — Brønsted-Lowry acids and bases, pH and pOH,
  relative strengths of acids and bases, hydrolysis of salts, polyprotic
  acids, buffers, and acid-base titrations.
- **Equilibria of Other Reaction Classes** — precipitation and dissolution,
  Lewis acids and bases, and coupled equilibria.
- **Thermodynamics** — spontaneity, entropy, the second and third laws of
  thermodynamics, and free energy.
- **Electrochemistry** — redox chemistry, galvanic cells, electrode and
  cell potentials, potential, free energy, and equilibrium, batteries and
  fuel cells, corrosion, and electrolysis.
- **Representative Metals, Metalloids, and Nonmetals** — periodicity, the
  representative metals, the metalloids, the nonmetals, and the occurrence,
  preparation, and properties of hydrogen, carbonates, nitrogen, phosphorus,
  oxygen, sulfur, the halogens, and the noble gases.
- **Transition Metals and Coordination Chemistry** — the transition metals
  and their compounds, coordination chemistry, and the spectroscopic and
  magnetic properties of coordination compounds.
- **Organic Chemistry** — hydrocarbons, alcohols and ethers, aldehydes,
  ketones, carboxylic acids, and esters, and amines and amides.
- **Nuclear Chemistry** — nuclear structure and stability, nuclear
  equations, radioactive decay, transmutation and nuclear energy, uses of
  radioisotopes, and the biological effects of radiation.
