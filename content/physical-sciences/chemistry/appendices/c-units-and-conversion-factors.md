*Source note:* the source prints the cubic foot as 28.316 L. A foot is exactly 0.3048 m, so a cubic foot is 28.3168… L, which is 28.317 L to the five figures printed here, the value the table of common conversion factors in [Section 1.6 Mathematical Treatment of Measurement Results](/physical-sciences/chemistry/01-essential-ideas/06-mathematical-treatment-of-measurement-results/) gives.

**Units of Mass**

| Unit | Equivalents |
|---|---|
| gram (g) | = 0.001 kg (exact, definition) |
| milligram (mg) | = 0.001 g (exact, definition) |
| kilogram (kg) | = 1000 g (exact, definition) |
| kilogram (kg) | = 2.205 lb\* |
| ton (metric) | = 1000 kg (exact, definition) |
| ton (metric) | = 2204.62 lb |
| ounce (oz) | = 28.35 g |
| pound (lb) | = 0.4535924 kg |
| ton (short) | = 2000 lb (exact, definition) |
| ton (short) | = 907.185 kg |
| ton (long) | = 2240 lb (exact, definition) |
| ton (long) | = 1.016 metric ton |

\* The ounce and pound are units of mass: the avoirdupois pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kg. The weight, $W$, of an object is a force equal to the product of mass and gravitational acceleration, $W = mg$, and the customary unit of force is a separate one, the pound-force.

*Source note:* the source’s footnote reads “Strictly speaking, the ounce and pound are units of weight, $W$ (a force equal to the product of mass and gravitational acceleration, $W = mg$). The conversion relations in this table are commonly used to equate masses and weight assuming a nominal value for $g$ at the surface of the earth.” The avoirdupois pound is a unit of mass; the National Institute of Standards and Technology’s [guide to the SI](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b9) lists the pound and the ounce under mass and the pound-force under force, and its [footnotes](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-footnotes) give the pound’s exact factor, 0.45359237 kg.

**Units of Energy**

| Unit | Equivalents |
|---|---|
| 4.184 joule (J) | = 1 thermochemical calorie (cal) |
| 1 thermochemical calorie (cal) | = $4.184 \times 10^{7 }$ erg |
| erg | = $10^{-7}$ J (exact, definition) |
| electron-volt (eV) | = $1.60218 \times 10^{-19}$ J = 23.061 $\text{kcal}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| liter∙atmosphere | = 24.217 cal = 101.325 J (exact, definition) |
| nutritional calorie (Cal) | = 1000 cal (exact, definition) = 4184 J |
| British thermal unit (BTU) | = 1054.804 J\* |

\* BTU is the amount of energy needed to heat one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit. Therefore, the exact relationship of BTU to joules and other energy units depends on the temperature at which BTU is measured. 59 °F (15 °C) is the most widely used reference temperature for BTU definition in the United States. At this temperature, the conversion factor is the one provided in this table.

**Units of Pressure**

| Unit | Equivalents |
|---|---|
| torr | = 1 mm Hg |
| pascal (Pa) | = $\text{N}\ \text{m}^{-2}$ (exact, definition) |
| pascal (Pa) | = $\text{kg}\ \text{m}^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-2}$ (exact, definition) |
| atmosphere (atm) | = 760 mm Hg |
| atmosphere (atm) | = 760 torr (exact, definition) |
| atmosphere (atm) | = 101,325 $\text{N}\ \text{m}^{-2}$ (exact, definition) |
| atmosphere (atm) | = 101,325 Pa (exact, definition) |
| bar | = $10^{5}$ Pa (exact, definition) |
| bar | = $10^{5}\ \text{kg}\ \text{m}^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-2}$ (exact, definition) |

*Source note:* the source marks the torr’s 1 mm Hg and the atmosphere’s 760 mm Hg “(exact, definition)”. Neither is exact. An atmosphere is defined as 101,325 Pa and a torr as $\frac{1}{760}$ of an atmosphere, while a millimeter of mercury is worked out separately, from the density of mercury and the acceleration of gravity, and comes out greater than a torr by about one part in seven million. The two rows stand here without the mark, since neither relation is exact; [Section 9.1 Gas Pressure](/physical-sciences/chemistry/09-gases/01-gas-pressure/) says of the torr that it “no longer corresponds exactly” to a millimeter of mercury.
