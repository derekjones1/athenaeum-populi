Coral reefs are home to about 25% of all marine species. They are being
threatened by climate change, oceanic acidification, and water pollution, all
of which change the composition of the solution known as seawater. Dissolved
oxygen in seawater is critical for sea creatures, but as the oceans warm,
oxygen becomes less soluble. As the concentration of carbon dioxide in the
atmosphere increases, the concentration of carbon dioxide in the oceans
increases, contributing to oceanic acidification. Coral reefs are particularly
sensitive to the acidification of the ocean, since the exoskeletons of the
coral polyps are soluble in acidic solutions. Humans contribute to the changing
of seawater composition by allowing agricultural runoff and other forms of
pollution to affect our oceans.

Solutions are crucial to the processes that sustain life and to many other
processes involving chemical reactions. This chapter considers the nature of
solutions and examines factors that determine whether a solution will form and
what properties it may have. The properties of colloids—mixtures containing
dispersed particles larger than the molecules and ions of typical
solutions—are also discussed.

## Sections

- **The Dissolution Process** — describing the basic properties of solutions and how they form; predicting whether a given mixture will yield a solution based on molecular properties of its components; and explaining why some solutions either produce or absorb heat when they form.
- **Electrolytes** — defining and giving examples of electrolytes; distinguishing between the physical and chemical changes that accompany dissolution of ionic and covalent electrolytes; and relating electrolyte strength to solute-solvent attractive forces.
