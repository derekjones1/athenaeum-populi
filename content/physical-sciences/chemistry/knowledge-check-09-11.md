### 9.4 Effusion and Diffusion of Gases

{{< selfcheck question="When a sample of gas is introduced to one part of a closed container—two bulbs joined by an open stopcock—its molecules disperse throughout the container. In a closed environment, diffusion will ultimately result in equal concentrations of gas throughout. The gaseous atoms and molecules continue to move, but since their concentrations are the same in both bulbs, the rates of transfer between the bulbs are equal (no *net* transfer of molecules occurs).

### 9.5 The Kinetic-Molecular Theory

{{< textin
  question="One postulate of the kinetic-molecular theory states that gas molecules exert no attractive or repulsive forces on each other or the container walls; therefore, their collisions are ________ (do not involve a loss of energy)."
  answer="elastic"
>}}

{{< selfcheck question="Use the kinetic-molecular theory to explain why the pressure exerted by a given amount of gas at a given temperature increases when the volume of the gas is decreased." >}}
If the gas volume of a given amount of gas at a given temperature is decreased (that is, if the gas is *compressed*), the molecules will be exposed to a decreased container wall area. Collisions with the container wall will therefore occur more frequently and the pressure exerted by the gas will increase.
===CHECKS===
the molecules will be exposed to a decreased container wall area
collisions with the container wall will therefore occur more frequently
{{< /selfcheck >}}

### 10.2 Properties of Liquids

{{< selfcheck question="Water forms drops on waxed surfaces and on many plastics such as polyethylene, but it spreads out on glass. Explain both behaviors in terms of adhesive and cohesive forces." >}}
Water forms drops on these surfaces because the cohesive forces within the drops are greater than the adhesive forces between the water and the plastic. Water spreads out on glass because the adhesive force between water and glass is greater than the cohesive forces within the water.

{{< selfcheck question="Dissolving a nonvolatile substance in a volatile liquid results in a lowering of the liquid’s vapor pressure. Explain this lowering in terms of the effect of the solute on the rates of the liquid’s vaporization and condensation." >}}
To vaporize, solvent molecules must be present at the surface of the solution. The presence of solute decreases the surface area available to solvent molecules and thereby reduces the rate of solvent vaporization. Since the rate of condensation is unaffected by the presence of solute, the net result is that the vaporization-condensation equilibrium is achieved with fewer solvent molecules in the vapor phase (i.e., at a lower vapor pressure).
===CHECKS===
the presence of solute decreases the surface area available to solvent molecules
reduces the rate of solvent vaporization
the rate of condensation is unaffected by the presence of solute
the vaporization-condensation equilibrium is achieved with fewer solvent molecules in the vapor phase
