In 1054, Chinese astronomers recorded the appearance of a “guest star” in the
sky, visible even during the day, which then disappeared slowly over the next
two years. The sudden appearance was due to a supernova explosion, which was
much brighter than the original star. Even though this supernova was observed
almost a millennium ago, the remaining Crab Nebula (shown in the image above)
continues to release energy today. It emits not only visible light but also
infrared light, X-rays, and other forms of electromagnetic radiation. The
nebula emits both continuous spectra (the blue-white glow) and atomic emission
spectra (the colored filaments). In this chapter, we will discuss light and
other forms of electromagnetic radiation and how they are related to the
electronic structure of atoms. We will also see how this radiation can be used
to identify elements, even from thousands of light years away.

## Sections

- **Electromagnetic Energy** — the basic behavior of waves, including travelling and standing waves; the wave nature of light; calculating frequency, wavelength, and energy; line and continuous emission spectra; and the particle nature of light.
- **The Bohr Model** — the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom, and using the Rydberg equation to calculate the energies of light emitted or absorbed by hydrogen atoms.
- **Development of Quantum Theory** — wave–particle duality extended to matter, the quantum mechanical description of electrons in an atom through three-dimensional wave functions, or orbitals, and the four quantum numbers that specify the state of an electron.
- **Electronic Structure of Atoms (Electron Configurations)** — deriving predicted ground-state electron configurations, the exceptions for atoms and ions, and relating electron configurations to the classification of elements in the periodic table.
- **Periodic Variations in Element Properties** — describing and explaining the trends in atomic size, ionization energy, and electron affinity of the elements.
