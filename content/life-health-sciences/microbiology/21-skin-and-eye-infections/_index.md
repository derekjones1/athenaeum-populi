The human body is covered in skin, and like most coverings, skin is
designed to protect what is underneath. One of its primary purposes is
to prevent microbes in the surrounding environment from invading
underlying tissues and organs. But in spite of its role as a protective
covering, skin is not itself immune from infection. Certain pathogens
and toxins can cause severe infections or reactions when they come in
contact with the skin. Other pathogens are opportunistic, breaching the
skin’s natural defenses through cuts, wounds, or a disruption of normal
microbiota resulting in an infection in the surrounding skin and
tissue. Still other pathogens enter the body via different routes—
through the respiratory or digestive systems, for example—but cause
reactions that manifest as skin rashes or lesions.

Nearly all humans experience skin infections to some degree. Many of
these conditions are, as the name suggests, “skin deep,” with symptoms
that are local and non-life-threatening. At some point, almost
everyone must endure conditions like acne, athlete’s foot, and minor
infections of cuts and abrasions, all of which result from infections
of the skin. But not all skin infections are quite so innocuous. Some
can become invasive, leading to systemic infection or spreading over
large areas of skin, potentially becoming life-threatening.

## Sections

- **Anatomy and Normal Microbiota of the Skin and Eyes** — the major
  anatomical features of the skin and eyes, how the resident
  microbiota of body sites such as the hands, back, feet, and eyes
  compare with one another, how microorganisms overcome the defenses
  of the skin and eyes to cause infection, and the general signs and
  symptoms of skin and eye infections.
- **Bacterial Infections of the Skin and Eyes** — staphylococcal skin
  infections including impetigo, streptococcal infections such as
  cellulitis, erysipelas, erythema nodosum, and necrotizing fasciitis,
  acne, cutaneous anthrax, and the bacterial eye infections
  conjunctivitis (including neonatal conjunctivitis and trachoma) and
  bacterial keratitis.
- **Viral Infections of the Skin and Eyes** — papillomas (warts) caused
  by human papillomavirus, oral herpes caused by herpes simplex virus,
  the childhood exanthems roseola and fifth disease, and viral
  conjunctivitis and herpes keratitis of the eye.
- **Mycoses of the Skin** — the cutaneous mycoses called tineas
  (ringworm), caused by dermatophyte molds, cutaneous aspergillosis,
  candidiasis of the skin and nails caused by *Candida albicans*, and
  the subcutaneous mycosis sporotrichosis.
- **Protozoan and Helminthic Infections of the Skin and Eyes** — the
  parasitic protozoan and helminthic infections for which the skin or
  eyes are the primary site of infection, including *Acanthamoeba*
  keratitis of the eye and loiasis, a filarial disease caused by the
  nematode *Loa loa*.
