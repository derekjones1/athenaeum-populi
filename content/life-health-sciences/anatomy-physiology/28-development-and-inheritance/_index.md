**Chapter Objectives**

After studying this chapter, you will be able to:

- List and explain the steps involved in fertilization
- Describe the major events in embryonic development
- Describe the major events in fetal development
- Discuss the adaptations of the body to pregnancy
- Describe the physiologic adjustments that the newborn must make in the first hours of extrauterine life
- Summarize the physiology of lactation
- Classify and describe the different patterns of inheritance
{{< /callout >}}

In approximately nine months, a single cell—a fertilized egg—develops into a fully formed infant consisting of trillions of cells with myriad specialized functions. The dramatic changes of fertilization, embryonic development, and fetal development are followed by remarkable adaptations of the newborn to life outside the womb. An offspring’s normal development depends upon the appropriate synthesis of structural and functional proteins. This, in turn, is governed by the genetic material inherited from the egg and sperm, as well as environmental factors.

## Sections

- **Fertilization** — the obstacles sperm must overcome to reach an oocyte, capacitation and its importance, and the events as a sperm fertilizes an oocyte.
- **Embryonic Development** — the stages before implantation, implantation, the four embryonic membranes, gastrulation, formation and functions of the placenta, the folding of the flat embryonic disc into a human form, and organogenesis.
- **Fetal Development** — how the embryonic and fetal periods differ, sexual differentiation, the fetal circulatory system and its shunts, and the course of fetal development from the end of the embryonic period to birth.
- **Changes During Pregnancy, Labor, and Birth** — how estrogen, progesterone, and hCG maintain pregnancy, the sources of weight gain, the maternal digestive, circulatory, and integumentary changes, the events leading to labor, and the three stages of childbirth.
- **Adjustments of the Infant at Birth and Postnatal Stages** — the importance of the first breath, the closing of the cardiac shunts, thermoregulation in the newborn, and the role of intestinal flora.
- **Lactation** — the structure of the lactating breast, the process of lactation, and how the composition of breast milk changes over the first days and within a single feeding.
- **Patterns of Inheritance** — genotype and phenotype, how alleles determine traits, Mendel’s experiments and human genetics, and the inheritance of autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, and sex-linked disorders.
