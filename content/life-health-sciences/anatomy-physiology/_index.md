Anatomy is the study of the body's structures and physiology the study of
how they work; this book teaches them together, system by system. It is the
two-semester course for nursing and allied-health students: it opens with
the levels of organization — the chemistry of the body, the cell, and the
four tissue types — then covers support and movement (skin, bone, joints,
and muscle), regulation and control (the nervous and endocrine systems),
fluids and transport (blood, the heart, the vessels, and the lymphatic and
immune systems), energy and exchange (respiration, digestion, metabolism,
the urinary system, and fluid and acid-base balance), and closes with
reproduction, development, and inheritance.

## Chapters

### Unit 1: Levels of Organization

- **An Introduction to the Human Body** — an overview of anatomy and
  physiology, the body's structural organization, the functions and
  requirements of human life, homeostasis, anatomical terminology, and
  medical imaging.
- **The Chemical Level of Organization** — the chemical composition of the
  human body: matter, elements, and subatomic particles; ionic, covalent,
  and hydrogen bonds; how chemical reactions store and release energy; and
  the inorganic and organic compounds essential to human functioning.
- **The Cellular Level of Organization** — the cell membrane and how it
  regulates what enters and leaves, the organelles of the cytoplasm, the
  nucleus and DNA replication, protein synthesis, the cell cycle and
  division, and cellular differentiation.
- **The Tissue Level of Organization** — how cells organize into tissues:
  the four main tissue types and their embryonic origins, tissue membranes,
  epithelial tissue and glands, connective, muscle, and nervous tissue, and
  how tissues respond to injury, aging, and cancer.

### Unit 2: Support and Movement

- **The Integumentary System** — the skin and its accessory structures: the
  layers of the skin and the hypodermis, hair, nails, and sweat and
  sebaceous glands, the functions of the skin including temperature
  regulation, and common diseases, disorders, and injuries of the skin and
  how it heals.
- **Bone Tissue and the Skeletal System** — the functions of the skeletal
  system, bone classification and structure, bone formation and
  development, fracture repair, the effects of exercise, nutrition, and
  hormones on bone, and calcium homeostasis.
- **Axial Skeleton** — the divisions of the skeletal system, the bones and
  openings of the skull, the regions and curves of the vertebral column, the
  thoracic cage, and the embryonic development of these structures.
- **The Appendicular Skeleton** — the pectoral girdle and bones of the upper
  limb, the pelvic girdle and pelvis, the bones of the lower limb, and the
  embryonic development of the limbs.
- **Joints** — the classification of joints, fibrous, cartilaginous, and
  synovial joints, the types of body movements, the anatomy of selected
  synovial joints, and the development of joints.
- **Muscle Tissue** — the three types of muscle tissue, skeletal muscle and
  its connective tissue layers, how a muscle fiber contracts and relaxes,
  the nervous system's control of muscle tension, the types of muscle fibers,
  exercise and muscle performance, cardiac muscle tissue, smooth muscle, and
  the development and regeneration of muscle tissue.
- **The Muscular System** — how skeletal muscles interact, their fascicle
  arrangements and lever systems, how muscles are named, the axial muscles
  of the head, neck, back, abdominal wall, and thorax, and the appendicular
  muscles of the pectoral girdle, upper limbs, pelvic girdle, and lower
  limbs.

### Unit 3: Regulation, Integration, and Control

- **The Nervous System and Nervous Tissue** — the basic structure and
  function of the nervous system, nervous tissue, the function of nervous
  tissue, the action potential, and communication between neurons.
- **Anatomy of the Nervous System** — the embryologic perspective, the
  central nervous system, circulation and the central nervous system, and
  the peripheral nervous system.
- **The Somatic Nervous System** — sensory perception, central processing,
  and motor responses.
- **The Autonomic Nervous System** — the divisions of the autonomic nervous
  system, autonomic reflexes and homeostasis, central control, and drugs
  that affect the autonomic system.
- **The Neurological Exam** — an overview of the neurological exam, the mental
  status exam, the cranial nerve exam, the sensory and motor exams, and the
  coordination and gait exams.
- **The Endocrine System** — an overview of the endocrine system, hormones,
  the pituitary gland and hypothalamus, the thyroid gland, the parathyroid
  glands, the adrenal glands, the pineal gland, gonadal and placental hormones,
  the endocrine pancreas, organs with secondary endocrine functions, and the
  development and aging of the endocrine system.

### Unit 4: Fluids and Transport

- **The Cardiovascular System: Blood** — an overview of blood, the production
  of the formed elements, erythrocytes, leukocytes and platelets, hemostasis,
  and blood typing.
- **The Cardiovascular System: The Heart** — heart anatomy, cardiac muscle
  and electrical activity, the cardiac cycle, cardiac physiology, and the
  development of the heart.
- **The Cardiovascular System: Blood Vessels and Circulation** — the structure
  and function of blood vessels, blood flow, blood pressure, and resistance,
  capillary exchange, homeostatic regulation of the vascular system,
  circulatory pathways, and the development of blood vessels and fetal
  circulation.
- **The Lymphatic and Immune System** — anatomy of the lymphatic and
  immune systems, barrier defenses and the innate immune response, the
  adaptive immune response: T lymphocytes and their functional types, the
  adaptive immune response: B-lymphocytes and antibodies, the immune
  response against pathogens, diseases associated with depressed or
  overactive immune responses, and transplantation and cancer immunology.
