**Chapter Objectives**

After studying this chapter, you will be able to:

- Compare and contrast the anatomical structure of arteries, arterioles, capillaries, venules, and veins
- Accurately describe the forces that account for capillary exchange
- List the major factors affecting blood flow, blood pressure, and resistance
- Describe how blood flow, blood pressure, and resistance interrelate
- Discuss how the neural and endocrine mechanisms maintain homeostasis within the blood vessels
- Describe the interaction of the cardiovascular system with other body systems
- Label the major blood vessels of the pulmonary and systemic circulations
- Identify and describe the hepatic portal system
- Describe the development of blood vessels and fetal circulation
- Compare fetal circulation to that of an individual after birth
{{< /callout >}}

In this chapter, you will learn about the vascular part of the cardiovascular
system, that is, the vessels that transport blood throughout the body and
provide the physical site where gases, nutrients, and other substances are
exchanged with body cells. When vessel functioning is reduced, blood-borne
substances do not circulate effectively throughout the body. As a result,
tissue injury occurs, metabolism is impaired, and the functions of every bodily
system are threatened.

## Sections

- **Structure and Function of Blood Vessels** — the three tunics that make up the walls of most blood vessels, how elastic arteries, muscular arteries, and arterioles differ in structure, location, and function, the structure of a capillary bed from the supplying metarteriole to the draining venule, and the structure and function of venous valves in the large veins of the extremities.
- **Blood Flow, Blood Pressure, and Resistance** — systolic pressure, diastolic pressure, pulse pressure, and mean arterial pressure, the clinical measurement of pulse and blood pressure, five variables affecting arterial blood flow and blood pressure, and several factors affecting blood flow in the venous system.
- **Capillary Exchange** — the primary mechanisms of capillary exchange, how capillary hydrostatic pressure and blood colloid osmotic pressure each contribute to net filtration pressure, filtration compared with reabsorption, and the fate of fluid that is not reabsorbed from the tissues into the vascular capillaries.
- **Homeostatic Regulation of the Vascular System** — the neural regulation of vascular homeostasis, the hormones that contribute to the renal regulation of blood pressure, the effects of exercise on vascular homeostasis, and how hypertension, hemorrhage, and circulatory shock affect vascular health.
- **Circulatory Pathways** — the vessels of the pulmonary circuit from the right ventricle to the left atrium, flow charts of the major systemic arteries from the aorta to the arteries feeding the upper and lower limbs, and of the major systemic veins from the feet to the right atrium.
- **Development of Blood Vessels and Fetal Circulation** — how blood vessels develop and how blood circulates in the fetus.
