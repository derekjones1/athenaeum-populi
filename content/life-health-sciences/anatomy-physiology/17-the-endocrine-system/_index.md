**Chapter Objectives**

After studying this chapter, you will be able to:

- Identify the contributions of the endocrine system to homeostasis
- Discuss the chemical composition of hormones and the mechanisms of hormone action
- Summarize the site of production, regulation, and effects of the hormones of the pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal, and pineal glands
- Discuss the hormonal regulation of the reproductive system
- Explain the role of the pancreatic endocrine cells in the regulation of blood glucose
- Identify the hormones released by the heart, kidneys, and other organs with secondary endocrine functions
- Discuss several common diseases associated with endocrine system dysfunction
- Discuss the embryonic development of, and the effects of aging on, the endocrine system
{{< /callout >}}

You may never have thought of it this way, but when you send a text message to
two friends to meet you at the dining hall at six, you’re sending digital
signals that (you hope) will affect their behavior—even though they are some
distance away. Similarly, certain cells send chemical signals to other cells in
the body that influence their behavior. This long-distance intercellular
communication, coordination, and control is critical for homeostasis, and it is
the fundamental function of the endocrine system.

## Sections

- **An Overview of the Endocrine System** — the types of intercellular communication and their importance, mechanisms, and effects, and the major organs and tissues of the endocrine system and where they lie in the body.
- **Hormones** — the three major classes of hormones by chemical structure, intracellular and cell membrane receptors, the cAMP and IP₃ signaling pathways, the factors that shape a target cell’s response, and how feedback loops and humoral, hormonal, and neural stimuli control hormone release.
- **The Pituitary Gland and Hypothalamus** — how the hypothalamus relates to the posterior and anterior lobes of the pituitary, the two hormones of the posterior pituitary, and the six hormones of the anterior pituitary with their target cells, principal actions, and regulation by the hypothalamus.
- **The Thyroid Gland** — the location and anatomy of the thyroid, the synthesis of triiodothyronine and thyroxine, the role of thyroid hormones in basal metabolism, and the hormone made by the parafollicular cells.
- **The Parathyroid Glands** — the location and structure of the parathyroid glands, the hormonal control of blood calcium levels, and the physiological response to parathyroid dysfunction.
- **The Adrenal Glands** — the location and structure of the adrenal glands, and the hormones of the adrenal cortex and adrenal medulla with their target cells and effects.
- **The Pineal Gland** — the location and structure of the pineal gland and the function of melatonin.
- **Gonadal and Placental Hormones** — the most important hormones produced by the testes and ovaries, and the hormones of the placenta and their functions.
- **The Endocrine Pancreas** — the location and structure of the pancreas, the morphology and function of the pancreatic islets, and how the functions of insulin and glucagon compare.
- **Organs with Secondary Endocrine Functions** — the organs that have a secondary endocrine function, the hormone each produces, and its effects.
- **Development and Aging of the Endocrine System** — the embryonic origins of the endocrine system and the effects of aging on it.
