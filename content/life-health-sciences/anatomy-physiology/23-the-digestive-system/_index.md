**Chapter Objectives**

After studying this chapter, you will be able to:

- List and describe the functional anatomy of the organs and accessory organs of the digestive system
- Discuss the processes and control of ingestion, propulsion, mechanical digestion, chemical digestion, absorption, and defecation
- Discuss the roles of the liver, pancreas, and gallbladder in digestion
- Compare and contrast the digestion of the three macronutrients
{{< /callout >}}

The digestive system is continually at work, yet people seldom appreciate the
complex tasks it performs in a choreographed biologic symphony. Consider what
happens when you eat an apple. Of course, you enjoy the apple’s taste as you
chew it, but in the hours that follow, unless something goes amiss and you get a
stomachache, you don’t notice that your digestive system is working. You may be
taking a walk or studying or sleeping, having forgotten all about the apple, but
your stomach and intestines are busy digesting it and absorbing its vitamins and
other nutrients. By the time any waste material is excreted, the body has
appropriated all it can use from the apple. In short, whether you pay attention
or not, the organs of the digestive system perform their specific functions,
allowing you to use the food you eat to keep you going. This chapter examines
the structure and functions of these organs, and explores the mechanics and
chemistry of the digestive processes.

## Sections

- **Overview of the Digestive System** — the organs of the alimentary canal from proximal to distal and their functions, the accessory digestive organs and their functions, the four fundamental tissue layers of the alimentary canal, the contributions of the enteric and autonomic nervous systems to digestive system functioning, and how the peritoneum anchors the digestive organs.
- **Digestive System Processes and Regulation** — the six fundamental activities of the digestive system, with an example of each, and how the neural and hormonal controls involved in digestion compare.
- **The Mouth, Pharynx, and Esophagus** — the structures of the mouth, including its three accessory digestive organs, the 32 adult teeth grouped by name, location, and function, the process of swallowing and the roles of the tongue, upper esophageal sphincter, and epiglottis, and the pathway food follows from ingestion into the mouth through release into the stomach.
- **The Stomach** — the four main regions of the stomach, its curvatures, and its sphincter, the four main types of secreting cells in gastric glands and their important products, why the stomach does not digest itself, and the mechanical and chemical digestion of food entering the stomach.
- **The Small and Large Intestines** — the location and gross anatomy of the small and large intestines compared, three main adaptations of the small intestine wall that increase its absorptive capacity, the mechanical and chemical digestion of chyme upon its release into the small intestine, three features unique to the wall of the large intestine and their contributions to its function, the beneficial roles of the bacterial flora, and the pathway of food waste from its entry into the large intestine through its exit from the body as feces.
- **Accessory Organs in Digestion: The Liver, Pancreas, and Gallbladder** — the main digestive roles of the liver, pancreas, and gallbladder, three main features of liver histology that are critical to its function, the composition and function of bile, and the major types of enzymes and buffers present in pancreatic juice.
- **Chemical Digestion and Absorption: A Closer Look** — the locations and primary secretions involved in the chemical digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids, and how absorption of the hydrophilic and hydrophobic nutrients compares.
