The arctic fox is an example of a complex animal that has adapted to its
environment and illustrates the relationships between an animal's form and
function. The structures of animals consist of primary tissues that make up
more complex organs and organ systems. Homeostasis allows an animal to
maintain a balance between its internal and external environments.

## Sections

- **Animal Form and Function** — the various types of body plans that
  occur in animals, the limits on animal size and shape, and how
  bioenergetics relates to body size, levels of activity, and the
  environment.
- **Animal Primary Tissues** — epithelial tissues, the different types of
  connective tissues in animals, the three types of muscle tissues, and
  nervous tissue.
- **Homeostasis** — the definition of homeostasis, the factors affecting
  it, positive and negative feedback mechanisms, and thermoregulation of
  endothermic and ectothermic animals.
