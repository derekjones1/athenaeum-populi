Despite its seeming hustle and bustle, Grand Central Station functions with
a high level of organization: People and objects move from one location to
another, they cross or are contained within certain boundaries, and they
provide a constant flow as part of larger activity. Analogously, a plasma
membrane’s functions involve movement within the cell and across boundaries'
activities. (credit: modification of work by Randy Le’Moine)
{{< /mediafigure >}}

The plasma membrane, the cell membrane, has many functions, but the most
basic one is to define the cell's borders and keep the cell functional. The
plasma membrane is selectively permeable. This means that the membrane
allows some materials to freely enter or leave the cell, while other
materials cannot move freely, but require a specialized structure, and
occasionally, even energy investment for crossing.

## Sections

- **Components and Structure** — the fluid mosaic model of the cell
  membrane, the roles of its phospholipids, proteins, and carbohydrates, and
  what makes a membrane fluid.
- **Passive Transport** — why and how passive transport occurs, the
  processes of osmosis and diffusion, and tonicity's role in passive
  transport.
- **Active Transport** — how electrochemical gradients affect ions and the
  difference between primary and secondary active transport.
- **Bulk Transport** — endocytosis — phagocytosis, pinocytosis, and
  receptor-mediated endocytosis — and the process of exocytosis.
