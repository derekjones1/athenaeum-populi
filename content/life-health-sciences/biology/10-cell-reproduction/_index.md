A human, like every sexually reproducing organism, begins life as a
fertilized egg (embryo) or **zygote**. In our species, billions of cell
divisions subsequently must occur in a controlled manner in order to produce
a complex, multicellular human comprising trillions of cells. Thus, the
original single-celled zygote is literally the ancestor of all cells in the
body. However, once a human is fully grown, cell reproduction is still
necessary to repair and regenerate tissues, and sometimes to increase our
size! In fact, all multicellular organisms use cell division for growth and
the maintenance and repair of cells and tissues. Cell division is closely
regulated, and the occasional failure of this regulation can have
life-threatening consequences. Single-celled organisms may also use cell
division as their method of reproduction.

## Sections

- **Cell Division** — the structure of prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes,
  how chromosomes, genes, and traits differ, and how chromosomes compact.
- **The Cell Cycle** — the three stages of interphase, how chromosomes
  behave during mitosis, how cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm, and the
  quiescent G0 phase.
- **Control of the Cell Cycle** — how internal and external mechanisms
  control the cell cycle, its three internal checkpoints, and the molecules
  that regulate it.
- **Cancer and the Cell Cycle** — how uncontrolled cell growth causes
  cancer, how mutated proto-oncogenes become oncogenes, and how tumor
  suppressors function and fail.
- **Prokaryotic Cell Division** — the process of binary fission in
  prokaryotes and how FtsZ and tubulin are homologous proteins.
