Imagine sailing down a river in a small motorboat on a weekend
afternoon; the water is smooth and you are enjoying the warm sunshine
and cool breeze when suddenly you are hit in the head by a 20-pound
silver carp. This is now a risk on many rivers and canal systems in
Illinois and Missouri because of the presence of Asian carp.

This fish—actually a group of species including the silver, black,
grass, and big head carp—has been farmed and eaten in China for over
1000 years. It is one of the most important aquaculture food resources
worldwide. In the United States, however, Asian carp is considered a
dangerous invasive species that disrupts community structure and
composition to the point of threatening native species.

## Sections

- **Population Demography** — how ecologists measure population size
  and density, the three patterns of population distribution, life
  tables and mortality rates, and the three types of survivorship
  curves.
- **Life Histories and Natural Selection** — how natural selection
  shapes life history patterns and how different reproductive
  strategies affect a species' survival.
- **Environmental Limits to Population Growth** — exponential and
  logistic population growth, examples of each in natural populations,
  and how natural selection and environmental adaptation shape life
  history patterns.
- **Population Dynamics and Regulation** — how a habitat's carrying
  capacity can change, density-dependent and density-independent
  population regulation, and examples of exponential and logistic
  growth in wild populations.
- **Human Population Growth** — exponential human population growth,
  how humans have expanded their habitat's carrying capacity, the
  relationship between population growth, age structure, and economic
  development, and the long-term implications of unchecked growth.
- **Community Ecology** — the predator-prey cycle, defenses against
  predation and herbivory, the competitive exclusion principle,
  symbiotic relationships between species, and community structure and
  succession.
- **Behavioral Biology: Proximate and Ultimate Causes of Behavior** —
  innate and learned behavior, movement and migration as products of
  natural selection, how populations communicate, mating displays and
  courtship behavior, the different mating systems, and how species
  learn.
