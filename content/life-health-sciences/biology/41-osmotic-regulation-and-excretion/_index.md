The daily intake recommendation for human water consumption is eight to
ten glasses of water. In order to achieve a healthy balance, the human
body should excrete the eight to ten glasses of water every day. This
occurs via the processes of urination, defecation, sweating and, to a
small extent, respiration. The organs and tissues of the human body are
soaked in fluids that are maintained at constant temperature, pH, and
solute concentration, all crucial elements of homeostasis. The solutes in
body fluids are mainly mineral salts and sugars, and osmotic regulation is
the process by which the mineral salts and water are kept in balance.
Osmotic homeostasis is maintained despite the influence of external
factors like temperature, diet, and weather conditions.

## Sections

- **Osmoregulation and Osmotic Balance** — osmosis and its role within the
  body, active transport mechanisms, osmolarity and how it is measured,
  and how osmoregulators and osmoconformers allow animals to adapt to
  different environments.
- **The Kidneys and Osmoregulatory Organs** — how the kidneys serve as the
  main osmoregulatory organs in mammalian systems, the structure of the
  kidney, the nephron as its functional unit, and the three steps in the
  formation of urine.
- **Excretion Systems** — how vacuoles in microorganisms, flame cells and
  nephridia in worms, and Malpighian tubules in insects excrete waste and
  maintain osmotic balance.
- **Nitrogenous Wastes** — how aquatic and terrestrial animals eliminate
  toxic ammonia, and how the major byproduct of ammonia metabolism
  compares between vertebrates and birds, insects, and reptiles.
- **Hormonal Control of Osmoregulatory Functions** — how hormonal cues
  help the kidneys synchronize the body's osmotic needs, including
  epinephrine, norepinephrine, renin-angiotensin, aldosterone,
  anti-diuretic hormone, and atrial natriuretic peptide.
