Vertebrates are among the most recognizable organisms of the Animal
Kingdom, and more than 62,000 vertebrate species have been identified. The
vertebrate species now living represent only a small portion of the
vertebrates that have existed in the past. The best-known extinct
vertebrates are the dinosaurs, a unique group of reptiles, some of which
reached sizes not seen before or after in *terrestrial* animals. In fact,
they were the dominant terrestrial animals for 150 million years, until
most of them died out in a mass extinction near the end of the Cretaceous
period (except for the feathered theropod ancestors of modern birds, whose
direct descendents now number nearly 10,000 species). Although it is not
known with certainty what caused this mass extinction (not only of
dinosaurs, but of many other groups of organisms), a great deal is known
about the anatomy of the dinosaurs and early birds, given the preservation
of numerous skeletal elements, nests, eggs, and embryos in the fossil
record.

## Sections

- **Chordates** — the distinguishing characteristics of chordates, the
  derived characters of craniates that set them apart from other
  chordates, and the developmental fate of the notochord in vertebrates.
- **Fishes** — the difference between jawless and jawed fishes, and the
  distinguishing features of sharks and rays compared to other modern
  fishes.
- **Amphibians** — the important difference between the life cycle of
  amphibians and the life cycles of other vertebrates, the
  characteristics that distinguish Urodela, Anura, and Apoda, and the
  evolutionary history of amphibians.
- **Reptiles** — the main characteristics of amniotes, the difference
  between anapsids, synapsids, and diapsids, the characteristics of
  reptiles, and the evolution of reptiles.
- **Birds** — the evolutionary history of birds and the derived
  characteristics that facilitate flight.
- **Mammals** — the distinguishing features of the three main groups of
  mammals, the likely line of evolutionary descent that produced mammals,
  derived features tied to mammals' need for constant, high-level
  metabolism, and the major clades of eutherian mammals.
- **The Evolution of Primates** — the derived features that distinguish
  primates from other animals, the defining features of the major primate
  groups, the major hominin precursors to modern humans, and why
  scientists are having difficulty determining the true lines of descent
  in hominids.
