## Simplify Expressions with $a^{\tfrac{1}{n}}$

Rational exponents are another way of writing expressions with radicals. When we use rational exponents, we can apply the properties of exponents to simplify expressions.

The Power Property for Exponents says that $\left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{m \cdot n}$ when *m* and *n* are whole numbers. Let’s assume we are now not limited to whole numbers.

Suppose we want to find a number *p* such that $\left(8^{p}\right)^{3} = 8.$ We will use the Power Property of Exponents to find the value of *p*.

$\begin{aligned} & & & & & \left(8^{p}\right)^{3} & = & 8\ \\ \text{Multiply the exponents on the left}. & & & & & 8^{3 p} & = & 8\ \\ \text{Write the exponent 1 on the right}. & & & & & 8^{3 p} & = & 8^{1}\ \\ \text{Since the bases are the same},\text{ the exponents must be equal}. & & & & & 3 p & = & 1\ \\ \text{Solve for}\ p. & & & & & p & = & \tfrac{1}{3}\end{aligned}$

So $\left(8^{\tfrac{1}{3}}\right)^{3} = 8.$ But we know also $\left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^{3} = 8.$ Then it must be that $8^{\tfrac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{8}.$

This same logic can be used for any positive integer exponent *n* to show that $a^{\tfrac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a}.$

### Rational Exponent $a^{\tfrac{1}{n}}$

If $\sqrt[n]{a}$ is a real number and $n \geq 2,$ then

$$a^{\tfrac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a}$$

The denominator of the rational exponent is the index of the radical.

There will be times when working with expressions will be easier if you use rational exponents and times when it will be easier if you use radicals. In the first few examples, you’ll practice converting expressions between these two notations.

**Example.**

Write as a radical expression: (a) $x^{\tfrac{1}{2}}$ (b) $y^{\tfrac{1}{3}}$ (c) $z^{\tfrac{1}{4}}.$

We want to write each expression in the form $\sqrt[n]{a}.$

(a)

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ |
| The denominator of the rational exponent is 2, sothe index of the radical is 2. We do not show theindex when it is 2. | $\sqrt{x}$ |

(b)

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $y^{\frac{1}{3}}$ |
| The denominator of the exponent is 3, so theindex is 3. | $\sqrt[3]{y}$ |

(c)

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $z^{\frac{1}{4}}$ |
| The denominator of the exponent is 4, so theindex is 4. | $\sqrt[4]{z}$ |

## Simplify Expressions with $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}}$

We can look at $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}}$ in two ways. Remember the Power Property tells us to multiply the exponents and so $\left(a^{\tfrac{1}{n}}\right)^{m}$ and $\left(a^{m}\right)^{^{\tfrac{1}{n}}}$ both equal $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}}.$ If we write these expressions in radical form, we get

$$a^{\tfrac{m}{n}} = \left(a^{\tfrac{1}{n}}\right)^{m} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m}\ \text{and}\ a^{\tfrac{m}{n}} = \left(a^{m}\right)^{^{\tfrac{1}{n}}} = \sqrt[n]{a^{m}}$$

This leads us to the following definition.

### Rational Exponent $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}}$

For any positive integers *m* and *n*,

$$a^{\tfrac{m}{n}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m}\ \text{and}\ a^{\tfrac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^{m}}$$

Which form do we use to simplify an expression? We usually take the root first—that way we keep the numbers in the radicand smaller, before raising it to the power indicated.

**Example.**

Write with a rational exponent: (a) $\sqrt{y^{3}}$ (b) $\left(\sqrt[3]{2 x}\right)^{4}$ (c) $\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{3 a}{4 b}\right)^{3}}.$

We want to use $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^{m}}$ to write each radical in the form $a^{\tfrac{m}{n}}.$

(a)

The exponent 3 on the radicand becomes the numerator of the rational exponent,
and the square root's index 2 becomes the denominator:

$$
\sqrt{y^3}=y^{\tfrac{3}{2}}.
$$

(b)

The outside exponent 4 becomes the numerator, and the cube root's index 3
becomes the denominator:

$$
\left(\sqrt[3]{2x}\right)^4=(2x)^{\tfrac{4}{3}}.
$$

(c)

The power 3 becomes the numerator, and the square root's index 2 becomes the
denominator:

$$
\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{3a}{4b}\right)^3}
=\left(\tfrac{3a}{4b}\right)^{\tfrac{3}{2}}.
$$

## Use the Properties of Exponents to Simplify Expressions with Rational Exponents

The same properties of exponents that we have already used also apply to rational exponents. We will list the Properties of Exponenets here to have them for reference as we simplify expressions.

### Properties of Exponents

If *a* and *b* are real numbers and *m* and *n* are rational numbers, then

$\begin{aligned}\textbf{Product Property} & & & a^{m} \cdot a^{n} & = & a^{m + n}\ \\ \textbf{Power Property} & & & \left(a^{m}\right)^{n} & = & a^{m \cdot n}\ \\ \textbf{Product to a Power} & & & \left(a b\right)^{m} & = & a^{m} b^{m}\ \\ \textbf{Quotient Property} & & & \tfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}} & = & a^{m - n}, a \neq 0\ \\ \textbf{Zero Exponent Definition} & & & a^{0} & = & 1, a \neq 0\ \\ \textbf{Quotient to a Power Property} & & & \left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^{m} & = & \tfrac{a^{m}}{b^{m}}, b \neq 0\ \\ \textbf{Negative Exponent Property} & & & a^{- n} & = & \tfrac{1}{a^{n}}, a \neq 0\end{aligned}$

We will apply these properties in the next example.

**Example.**

Simplify: (a) $x^{\tfrac{1}{2}} \cdot x^{\tfrac{5}{6}}$ (b) $\left(z^{9}\right)^{\tfrac{2}{3}}$ (c) $\tfrac{x^{\tfrac{1}{3}}}{x^{\tfrac{5}{3}}}.$

(a) The Product Property tells us that when we multiply the same base, we add the exponents.

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $x^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot x^{\frac{5}{6}}$ |
| The bases are the same, so we add the exponents. | $x^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{5}{6}}$ |
| Add the fractions. | $x^{\frac{8}{6}}$ |
| Simplify the exponent. | $x^{\frac{4}{3}}$ |

(b) The Power Property tells us that when we raise a power to a power, we multiply the exponents.

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $\left(z^{9}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$ |
| To raise a power to a power, we multiplythe exponents. | $z^{9 \cdot \frac{2}{3}}$ |
| Simplify. | $z^{6}$ |

(c) The Quotient Property tells us that when we divide with the same base, we subtract the exponents.

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
|  | $\frac{x^{\frac{1}{3}}}{x^{\frac{5}{3}}}$ |
|  | $\frac{x^{\frac{1}{3}}}{x^{\frac{5}{3}}}$ |
| To divide with the same base, we subtractthe exponents. | $\frac{1}{x^{\frac{5}{3} - \frac{1}{3}}}$ |
| Simplify. | $\frac{1}{x^{\frac{4}{3}}}$ |
