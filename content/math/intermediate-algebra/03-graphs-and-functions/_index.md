Imagine visiting a faraway city or even outer space from the comfort of your
living room. It could be possible using virtual reality. This technology
creates realistic images that make you feel as if you are truly immersed in
the scene and even enable you to interact with them. It is being developed
for fun applications, such as video games, but also for architects to plan
buildings, car companies to design prototypes, the military to train, and
medical students to learn.

Developing virtual reality devices requires modeling the environment using
graphs and mathematical relationships. In this chapter, you will graph
different relationships and learn ways to describe and analyze graphs.

## Sections

- **Graph Linear Equations in Two Variables** — plotting points, graphing
  linear equations, graphing vertical and horizontal lines, and finding and
  using intercepts.
- **Slope of a Line** — finding and using slope, graphing lines from a point or
  an intercept, interpreting applications, and identifying parallel and
  perpendicular lines.
- **Find the Equation of a Line** — finding equations of lines from a slope
  and intercept, a slope and point, or two points, including parallel and
  perpendicular lines.
- **Graph Linear Inequalities in Two Variables** — verifying solutions,
  relating solutions to their graphs, graphing linear inequalities, and using
  them in applications.
- **Relations and Functions** — finding the domain and range of a relation,
  determining whether a relation is a function, and evaluating functions.
- **Graphs of Functions** — using the vertical line test, identifying graphs
  of basic functions, and reading domain, range, intercepts, and function
  values from graphs.
